Frequently Asked Questions

What is ExoDetect?

ExoDetect is an advanced, web-based astrophysical analyzer designed to process transit light curves from NASA's Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) mission. It cleans the data and employs algorithms to discover and validate potential exoplanetary signatures.

What is a TIC ID and where do I find one?

The TESS Input Catalog (TIC) ID is a unique numeric identifier assigned to stars monitored by the TESS spacecraft. You can find TIC IDs by searching astronomical catalogs, platforms like ExoFOP, or scientific papers. A few popular target stars to analyze are: 141872132, 25155310, or 100100827.

How does the Box Least Squares (BLS) search work?

The Box Least Squares (BLS) algorithm is a statistical technique optimized for detecting periodic, box-like brightness drops (transits). It scans a range of candidate orbital periods and transit durations to locate the strongest periodic dip in the light curve.

What does the Confidence Score tell us?

The confidence score is computed dynamically based on the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR), transit depth realism, odd-even consistency, secondary eclipse depths, and transit coverage data. A higher score represents a signal profile that strongly mirrors a real exoplanet candidates and has low false-positive likelihood.

How can we identify Eclipsing Binaries or False Positives?

Eclipsing binaries (two stars orbiting each other) mimic planetary transits but have distinct signatures. ExoDetect identifies them by measuring depth differences between alternating (odd and even) transits, and by searching for a secondary eclipse (a shallow secondary dip when the smaller star goes behind the larger star).

Where is the stellar data retrieved from?

Stellar light curves are queried and downloaded in real-time from the Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST), which hosts public space science data products from Kepler, K2, and TESS telescopes funded by NASA.

About ExoDetect

ExoDetect is a state-of-the-art NASA TESS Exoplanet Transit Analyzer. Our mission is to democratize citizen science by providing powerful tools to explore, clean, and analyze stellar light curves directly from your browser.

How it Works

By entering a TESS Input Catalog (TIC) ID, ExoDetect queries real-time light curve databases from the Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST). The system then cleans instrumental trends, detrends the data, and runs a Box Least Squares (BLS) periodogram search to detect planetary transits.

Vetting & Verification

Each potential signal undergoes strict physical vetting, checking for eclipsing binaries (via odd-even depth differences and secondary eclipse searches) and fitting U-shape vs. V-shape transit profiles to ensure reliable confidence scoring.
